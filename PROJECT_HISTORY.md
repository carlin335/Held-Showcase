# Held project history

Held is an actively developed private iPhone prototype. This public timeline records major product milestones without publishing implementation details.

**Development began August 16, 2026.** The complete dated source history is retained privately; this public repository records product milestones only.

## August 2026

### Held 2.3.1 — Interface and valuation reliability

- Corrected the primary navigation layout.
- Added stronger Pokémon set-aware matching.
- Expanded legitimate market-source fallbacks when a direct value was unavailable.

### Held 2.4 — Precision scanner

- Introduced stable-frame confirmation and repeated-reading agreement.
- Improved close-focus camera behavior, glare handling, and low-light capture.
- Added category-specific recognition for cards, coins, wine, and other collectibles.

### Held 2.4.1 — Exact Pokémon printing

- Improved tiny collector-number capture.
- Preserved full fractions such as `021/086` for set-level verification.
- Prevented a name-only Pokémon reading from being presented as an exact priced match.

### Held 2.4.2 — Special Pokémon titles

- Expanded recognition for ex, EX, V, VMAX, VSTAR, V-UNION, GX, BREAK, LV.X, Radiant, Mega, Tera, and related printed-name forms.
- Improved long-title capture without weakening collector-number verification.

### Held 2.4.3 — Sports-card recognition

- Added recognition for player, year, set, card number, rookie, autograph, parallel, serial-number, and vertical-text clues.
- Improved candidate ranking from the evidence printed on the card.

### Held 2.4.4 — Real sports-card candidate search

- Replaced generic player-name placeholders with real catalogue candidate lists.
- Added year, release, set, and card-number context to typed sports-card searches.
- Preserved explicit setup and error states when a catalogue connection is unavailable.

## Continuing development

Current priorities are faster exact identification, stronger variation handling, real-device validation, provider reliability, and a production-safe service architecture. The implementation and detailed commit history are maintained privately.
